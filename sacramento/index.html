Keeping Your Sacramento Water Heater Running Longer

Most early failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms like discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a surprise cleanup.
