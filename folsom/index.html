Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Folsom

Many early failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually includes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows discolored water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone today so you can reach us fast as soon as something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Hot Water Headaches Folsom Homeowners Face

Water heaters in and around Folsom deal with a familiar set of problems. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, hard water may be part of the picture: dissolved minerals drop out as sediment on the tank floor, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the rumbling and popping many homeowners hear when the heater fires. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, and that's why annual descaling matters so much in hard-water areas.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A standard tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, and near the end of that window the anode rod is often eaten away, leaving the steel open to rust. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Setup matters too. A home on a private well can see more sediment and occasionally a sulfur smell in the hot water, which a different anode rod often cures. A tankless heater in hard water needs regular descaling. And an older installation being replaced may need updates it never had: an expansion tank, a properly routed relief valve line, a drain pan, or seismic straps where required. Every one of these problems can be fixed, and most cost far less to address early.
