Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Signs of scorching or backdrafting around the base or top of a gas heater
  • No hot water at all, even after waiting for the tank to recover
  • The smell of gas near the unit, a situation that calls for evacuating and phoning the utility or 911
  • The relief valve on the side of the tank dripping, hissing, or discharging
  • Sudden loud knocking or boiling sounds coming from inside the tank
  • A heater breaker that will not stay on after being reset
  • Standing water around the water heater that returns after you wipe it up
  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot

The Benefits of Professional Emergency Water Heater Repair

It is tempting to treat a leaking or dead water heater as a quick fix. In reality, you may be dealing with standing water beside 240-volt wiring, a gas supply and open burner a few inches from a puddle, and a tank of very hot water under pressure. The relief valve, if it is firing, is the last safeguard between an overheated tank and a violent failure, and plugging it is one of the most dangerous things anyone can do to a heater. Relighting a pilot while gas lingers, or forcing a rusted connection, can make things much worse. Evacuate for gas, isolate, then phone is the right sequence for anyone without the tools and training.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and the right parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution rather than guessing, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Just as important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a few hundred dollars versus a new heater. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Here's where to begin:

  • Keep the thermostat near 120F to limit scalding and reduce strain on the tank
  • Consider an automatic leak shutoff that stops the cold supply the moment water hits the floor
  • A drain pan with a proper drain line under the tank can turn a flood into a manageable puddle
  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • Flush sediment from the tank yearly, since buildup overheats the bottom and speeds corrosion
  • Find and label the cold-water shutoff at the heater and the main house valve now, before you ever need them
  • Test the T&P valve annually; a valve that sticks or weeps afterward should be replaced
